## Proposed bounded security contract — for independent approval, not yet accepted

1. **Authority before effect entry:** Restricted worker effects require current run/session/worker/attempt/assignment/scope/invocation identity and a single-use durable approval claim. A changed or missing identity fails closed before downstream dispatch.
2. **After effect entry:** Cancellation and revocation are cooperative. ALiX does **not** guarantee rollback or immediate cancellation of an arbitrary downstream tool that has already entered its router/adapter.
3. **Ambiguous outcomes:** An effect that might have executed but lacks a durable success receipt remains unknown/pending; the same uncertain effect must **not** be automatically retried or inferred failed/succeeded. Reconciliation requires an operator or a separately proven adapter idempotency contract. This is a release control to verify, **not** a claim that all bypass routes or fresh invocation identities are already protected.
4. **Replay:** Approval single-use and invocation binding do not guarantee exactly-once shell/API/plugin/provider effects. `RISK-REPLAY-01` stays OPEN, including bypass paths and new invocation identities.
5. **Daemon admission:** Exclusivity and lifecycle publication rely on participating binaries, portable fail-closed locks and verified owner identity. Malformed/unprovable ownership may require documented manual recovery; no unsafe automatic lock stealing.
6. **Supported evidence scope:** Linux and the configured macOS CI daemon suites are qualified at the pinned SHA. Windows named-pipe daemon lifecycle, arbitrary power-loss durability, same-user hostile socket impersonation and external-provider exactly-once are **not qualified**.

**Release prerequisite proposal:** Any high-impact non-idempotent tool must be configured with (a) adapter-level idempotency and a verified reconciliation key, or (b) a human reconciliation/approval workflow after unknown outcome; no unattended automatic replay after uncertainty. This is a proposed hardening requirement, not evidence that enforcement exists across all tool paths. The operator must decide whether enforcement is required before Phase F sign-off or tracked as a release blocker.
