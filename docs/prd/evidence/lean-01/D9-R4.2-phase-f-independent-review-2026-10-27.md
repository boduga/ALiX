1. **HIGH — Another worker can forge the successful receipt used to requeue a D9 worker.**

   [`recordExecutionOutcome`, lines 797–804](/home/babasola/Projects/ALiX-d9-crash-probe/src/governance/approvals/approval-store.ts:797) checks the reporting worker against `record.workerId` only when that field exists. It does not check `consumedByWorkerId` or `consumedAttempt`. The [worker port](/home/babasola/Projects/ALiX-d9-crash-probe/src/governance/approvals/worker-approval-port.ts:198) exposes this operation.

   D9 supports initially unbound tool approvals: its [acceptance fixture](/home/babasola/Projects/ALiX-d9-crash-probe/tests/security/daemon-tool-continuation-acceptance.vitest.ts:33) creates one, and the resumer adds `toolContinuation` and consumption identity without setting `workerId`. Consequently, a different worker holding a restricted port can change this consumed approval from `pending` to `succeeded`.

   [`recoverSucceeded`](/home/babasola/Projects/ALiX-d9-crash-probe/src/operations/daemon/tool-continuation-resumer.ts:183) then accepts that receipt and requeues the original worker. Its consumer checks cannot establish who reported the outcome.

   An isolated probe using the real source port, outcome method, and recovery method produced:
   ```text
   reporter=worker-b consumer=worker-a reported=true
   outcome=succeeded workerStatus=pending toolExecuted=false
   ```
   Storage mutation, committed reads, and run locking were mocked; this establishes the authorization defect, not persistence durability. The existing [outcome parity table](/home/babasola/Projects/ALiX-d9-crash-probe/tests/security/worker-approval-atomic-outcome-acceptance.vitest.ts:50) explicitly allows all callers for unbound records and misses their later D9 attribution.

2. **HIGH — Cancellation/context loss can occur before dispatch without preventing the write.**

   The resumer’s [final worker check](/home/babasola/Projects/ALiX-d9-crash-probe/src/operations/daemon/tool-continuation-resumer.ts:155) precedes asynchronous continuation loading, ownership checks, approval consumption, and telemetry. Its [monitor](/home/babasola/Projects/ALiX-d9-crash-probe/src/operations/daemon/tool-continuation-resumer.ts:143) aborts a signal, but the executor’s [continuation branch, lines 536–558](/home/babasola/Projects/ALiX-d9-crash-probe/src/capabilities/tools/executor.ts:536) does not recheck that signal before consuming or dispatching. The [`file.create` router](/home/babasola/Projects/ALiX-d9-crash-probe/src/capabilities/tools/tool-router.ts:186) also performs its write without checking cancellation.

   Thus cancellation or shutdown after the initial check—even when already observed through an aborted signal—can still launch a filesystem effect. Changes to worker scope/state during this interval likewise lack atomic validation at dispatch.

   An isolated probe of the exact source dispatch method aborted during `tool.started`, before downstream execution:
   ```text
   routerCalls=1 result.kind=success receivedAbortedSignal=true
   ```
   Authority and router dependencies were mocked; no filesystem effect was performed.
