## Decision (2026-09-30): instrumentation-only, no blind labelling

Cohort D is closed as **instrumentation evidence, not a selection-quality
measurement**. Recorded here so the corpus is not later mistaken for an
un-analysed checkpoint.

Track A cannot be run on this cohort. Blind labelling requires scopes where the
model's choice and Jev's top pick DIFFER; D has exactly **one**:

```text
scope_1  "Which MCP servers are configured here, and what does each expose?"
         actual = builtin:alix_grep_search
         jev    = builtin:alix_mcp_search_tools
```

17 of 18 scopes agree. The preregistered checkpoint needs 30 eligible scopes AND
10 labelled disagreements; D supplies 18 scopes and 1. Labelling what exists
would produce a record far below the threshold and could not support any T4
recommendation, so no `labels.jsonl` is written for this cohort.

What D DOES establish, which is the point of it:

1. The two F4 bypasses are closed — 18 external scopes exist where cohort C had
   zero, across both the grounded route and the `alix_mcp_search_tools` sentinel.
2. No raw MCP handle appears in any identity field (0 hits, identity-field scan).
3. Candidate sets are preserved 18/18, so the comparison machinery is sound even
   where the sample is too small to conclude.

The high agreement is NOT evidence that Jev is good, for the same reason cohort
C's balance was not evidence that it is bad: on a 2–6 candidate external surface
the deterministic top pick and the model's choice coincide most of the time. It
is an uninformative sample, not a favourable one. Reading it as a win would be
the same error as reading cohort C as a loss.

To make external selection measurable, a new cohort is required with enough
external scopes to produce ~10 disagreements — which on this agreement rate
means on the order of 150+ external scopes. That is a scoping decision, not a
data-collection one, and it is out of scope here.
